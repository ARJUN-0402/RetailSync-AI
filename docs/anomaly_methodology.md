# Anomaly Detection Methodology

## Overview

This document describes the demand anomaly detection pipeline for RetailSync AI.

**Selected Method:** Ensemble (requires 2+ method agreement)
**Anomaly Rate:** 8.66% of all records (31,619 anomalies out of 365,000)
**Output:** `data/processed/anomalies.csv`, `data/processed/features_with_anomalies.csv`

---

## Methods Compared

### 1. Rolling Z-Score
- **Window:** 30 days (shifted by 1 to avoid leakage)
- **Thresholds:** |Z| > 2 = Unusual, |Z| > 3 = Significant
- **Results:** 29,779 anomalies (8.16%)
- **Pros:** Simple, interpretable, captures deviations from local mean
- **Cons:** Sensitive to window size choice

### 2. IQR Method
- **Window:** 30 days (shifted by 1)
- **Thresholds:** Q1 - 1.5*IQR, Q3 + 1.5*IQR
- **Results:** 67,816 anomalies (18.58%)
- **Pros:** Robust to outliers, distribution-free
- **Cons:** Higher false positive rate

### 3. Isolation Forest
- **Features:** Product-store aggregates (mean, std, max, min, CV, coverage)
- **Contamination:** 5%
- **Results:** 18,250 anomalies (5.00%)
- **Pros:** Captures multivariate anomalies, fast
- **Cons:** Less interpretable, requires feature engineering

### 4. Ensemble (Selected)
- **Rule:** Flag as anomaly if 2+ methods agree
- **Results:** 31,619 anomalies (8.66%)
- **Breakdown:**
  - Anomaly (2+ methods): 31,619
  - Suspicious (1 method): 51,120
  - Normal (0 methods): 282,261

## Anomaly Categories

### Demand Spike
- **Definition:** `z_score > 2` AND `quantity_sold > iqr_upper`
- **Count:** 28,292 (89.4% of anomalies)
- **Interpretation:** Unusually high demand for a product-store combination

### Demand Drop
- **Definition:** `z_score < -2` AND `quantity_sold < iqr_lower`
- **Count:** 0 (0% of anomalies)
- **Interpretation:** Unusually low demand

### Unusual Pattern
- **Definition:** Flagged by Isolation Forest but not by Z-score or IQR
- **Count:** 3,327 (10.6% of anomalies)
- **Interpretation:** Multivariate anomaly not captured by univariate methods

## Business Interpretation

### What happened?
- Demand spikes represent periods where actual sales significantly exceeded expected levels
- These may be caused by: promotions, local events, supply chain disruptions, or viral trends

### When?
- Anomalies are distributed throughout the 2-year period
- Peaks often align with holiday seasons (November-December for Electronics)

### Where?
- Urban stores (Phoenix, San Jose) show more anomalies than Rural stores
- Electronics category has highest anomaly concentration

### Magnitude?
- Z-scores range from 2.0 to 7.0+
- Highest anomaly: Z = 7.0 (more than 7 standard deviations above mean)

### Why it matters?
- Stockouts during demand spikes lead to lost revenue
- Overstocking for spikes increases holding costs
- Understanding spike patterns improves forecasting and inventory planning

## Limitations

1. **Zero-inflation bias:** High proportion of zero-demand days makes baseline statistics unstable, potentially inflating anomaly counts.

2. **No causal attribution:** The pipeline identifies anomalies but does not explain their root cause (e.g., promotion, holiday, supply issue).

3. **Fixed thresholds:** Z-score and IQR thresholds are static. Adaptive thresholds based on product characteristics would improve accuracy.

4. **No seasonal adjustment:** Anomalies during known high-demand periods (e.g., holidays) may be false positives.
